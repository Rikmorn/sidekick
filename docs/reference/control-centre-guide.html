The control centre, row by row

sidekick adds a few rows to the Claude Code session you work in: who needs you, what your workers are doing, how the board stands, and which profile is loaded. You move between sessions with Claude Code's own agent view.

Every screen here is a real session, captured and then redrawn. sidekick's views come from Claude Code 2.1.310 with sidekick at 31b5826, and the desk's three screens from 2.1.311 at b9b39b0. Agent view, the typeahead, the permission prompt, Ctrl+Z, and the folded band come from 2.1.309 at 66ed52e. Your colours follow your terminal theme. Session names and paths from other work are replaced with neutral ones.

1The model

Run one main per terminal tab. It is the conversation you work in, and the only kind of session that draws the desk and the board. Everything else runs in the background, and agent view is how you reach it.

RoleWhat it isWhat sidekick draws there
MainThe session you work in, before and after ←The desk's band and pane, the board's line and pane, and the identity row
WorkerA session your main dispatched, in its own worktreeThe identity row, which names its main and the keys back
OtherAny other background session, such as a bare claude --bgThe identity row alone

← works on an empty prompt. The first time your main launches a worker, sidekick fixes the main's name with /rename once the session is idle. That keeps the name the worker points back to. Press ← before then and the name is lost for that worker. A name you gave with -n or /rename already holds, and sidekick leaves it alone.

The identity row sits under the prompt in every session that runs sidekick's mods. It gives the session's name, its role, and its profile, or no profile.

2Your main at a glance

With two workers out, one stopped on a permission prompt, and another main waiting on a question, the bottom of your main looks like this.

The board's line

In a repository tracked on a GitHub board, one line under the identity row says how the milestone stands.

◆ on track
The last status update says so, and nothing has drifted.
✓ complete
The milestone is done.
▲ at risk
The last status update flags a risk.
▲ drift · …
The board and the repository disagree, such as unpushed commits or a stale card. The detail names it.
▼ off track
You marked the milestone off track.
○ inactive, …
The last status update marks the milestone inactive. ○ also leads the words for what the line lacks, such as no update or no milestone.

The line refreshes when a session starts, when /board opens, and after a commit, a push, or a board write. It has no clock. When a refresh fails, the time reads refresh failed HH:MM and the rest is the last good reading.

When another main waits on you

Your band names every session that waits on you, whichever main it belongs to. Here a second main, other-main, sees demo-main and its worker waiting.

A main that ends its turn on a question counts as waiting too. The engine reads such a turn as blocked, as other-main · blocked shows in the first picture of §2. It can misread a very short reply the same way.

3Panes and commands

Each command shows or hides its pane. Below 110 columns, or on the classic screen, a pane sits inline above the prompt. In fullscreen from 110 columns it docks on the right.

Docked, the desk and the board ask for 40 columns, but a width you kept wins. Claude Code keeps one dock width for every plugin's pane: the last one you dragged or keyed. Widen a focused pane with Ctrl+X ←.

CommandShowsKeysCloses with
/deskA tree of sessions: those elsewhere that need you, this main, its workers, then the rest, each with what it does and its contextStay with the prompt/desk again
/boardThe milestone as a plan tree, under a link to the board: its state, next move, drift, and outcome, then its plans and their issuesStay with the prompt/board again
/profileThe profile, what it hides, and what loaded into contextTaken by the pane: ↓ scrolls at onceEsc, or /profile again

A pane that holds the keyboard draws its frame bright, or its grip when docked. To scroll /desk or /board, press Ctrl+X Tab to give them the keys, then ↓. Esc hands the keys back and leaves the pane open. A pane with more rows than room says so in its first row.

With two panes docked, each Ctrl+X Tab gives the keys to the next pane and brings it to the front. The press after the last pane hands the keys back to the prompt.

4Getting around

← on an empty prompt opens agent view, with the session you left selected.

KeyWhereWhat it does
←Any session, empty promptOpens agent view. In a session you are attached to, it detaches and starts nothing.
EnterAgent viewOpens the selected session. On a group header it folds or unfolds the group.
EscAgent view, right after ←Returns to your conversation, now running in the background process, like Enter.
↑ ↓Agent viewMoves between rows. Rows reorder as their states change.
Alt+1…Alt+9Agent viewOpens session N in this folder. Pin your main with Ctrl+T so Alt+1 is always your main.
Ctrl+XAgent viewStops the selected session. A second press soon after deletes it, worktree included. Check the selection first.
Ctrl+C twiceAnywhereQuits the tab. Background sessions, your main among them, keep running.
claude attach <name>A shellOpens a running session by name or part of it. A stopped one opens by id only.
/stopAn attached sessionStops it, keeping its transcript and worktree. /exit only detaches.

Claude Code's agent view documentation has the full list. An idle background session stops after about an hour unless pinned, and resumes when you open it.

Ctrl+Z does two different things

In a session you started in the terminal, Ctrl+Z suspends Claude Code to the shell. Type fg to bring it back, views and all.

In a session opened with claude attach, Ctrl+Z closes the attach and the session runs on in the background. Nothing is left stopped.

The /desktop trap

Claude Code has its own /desktop command, which hands the session to the Claude desktop app. Where sidekick's /desk is registered, it lists first. Where it is not, /desktop lists first, and Enter runs it.

5Working with workers

A worker runs one plan in its own worktree while you keep working in your main. You choose the worker mode when a plan is ready; the main does the rest.

StepWhat happensWhat you see
DispatchThe main writes a brief and runs sidekick worker dispatchThe launch output, and once the main is idle, a /rename that fixes its name, unless you named it
WatchWithin 10 seconds the worker joins the deskThe band's workers line. In fullscreen from 144 columns, the desk pane opens by itself
QuestionsThe worker messages its questions to the main and waitsThe message in the main's transcript. A permission prompt shows under needs you. When the two sessions' permission modes differ in class, the message waits behind a dialog. Choose Deliver, since Deny is the default
Look in←, select the worker, EnterThe worker's identity row, which names its main and the keys back
SteerThe main writes a ruling to the rulings file and messages the workerThe worker's transcript, once you look in
FinishThe main reviews the branch, integrates it, and runs sidekick worker finishThe report saved; the worker leaves the desk

7Settle by use

These are open on purpose. Try each while you work, then tell any main what you found: “file this: …”. Each finding becomes its own issue.

The control centre's shape

The commands
Do all three of /desk, /board, and /profile earn their place? Would the desk serve you better always open?
The keys
/profile takes the keys when it opens; the other two leave them with the prompt. Which feels right?
The desk's order
The tree leads with sessions that need you, and its first row counts any needs-you row the room hides. Does a waiting session still get lost?
Which main
The identity row names each session, and a main's says your main. Is that enough to tell your mains apart?
The identity row
Every session draws one row under the prompt, with or without a profile. Useful, or noise?

Only your terminal can show these

The link
Cmd-click board #2 on the board's line. Does it open the board?
The pane's link
Click board #2 in the docked board pane's header, then Cmd-click it. Does either open the board?
The tabs
With two panes docked, click the tab that is not shown. Does it bring that pane to the front?
Alt+1
In agent view, does Alt+1 reach your pinned main, or does Option type a character?
Themes
Do the band's colours read in your other themes?
The desktop app
Open a main in the Claude desktop app. What of the control centre shows there?
